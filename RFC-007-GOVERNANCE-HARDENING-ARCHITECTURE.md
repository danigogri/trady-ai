# RFC-007-GOVERNANCE-HARDENING-ARCHITECTURE

## Purpose

Define the official governance architecture, authority boundaries, operational sovereignty doctrine, approval hierarchy, freeze discipline, and bounded autonomy principles governing Trady AI.

This RFC establishes the official governance architecture for:

* operational authority
* deployment authorization
* freeze enforcement
* rollback governance
* risk authorization
* lifecycle approval
* bounded autonomy control
* governance continuity

This document defines:

* governance doctrine
* authority hierarchy
* approval philosophy
* operational sovereignty
* freeze governance
* escalation boundaries
* rollback authority
* forbidden governance behaviors

This RFC does NOT define:

* authentication systems
* permission infrastructure
* IAM implementation
* deployment tooling
* secret management
* dashboards
* infrastructure security implementation

Those belong to SDD-level documents.

---

# Core Principle

```text id="t8a3h2"
Governance authorizes.
The system executes.
```

Governance remains the highest operational authority inside Trady AI.

Execution systems never inherit unrestricted governance authority.

---

# Architectural Identity

Governance systems are:

* authoritative
* externalized
* bounded
* review-oriented
* freeze-preserving
* lifecycle-aware
* operationally sovereign

Governance systems are NOT:

* execution engines
* autonomous intelligence systems
* orchestration runtimes
* probabilistic authority systems
* hidden approval systems
* implicit deployment mechanisms

---

# Official Governance Doctrine

Governance exists to:

* preserve operational integrity
* authorize lifecycle transitions
* approve bounded evolution
* preserve freeze discipline
* constrain operational risk
* externalize authority
* preserve architectural stability

Governance owns:

* approval authority
* freeze authority
* rollback authority
* deployment authority
* capital authorization
* risk authorization
* lifecycle progression authority

Governance is the ultimate operational constraint layer.

---

Official authority hierarchy:

```text id="6lvn6r"
Governance
↓
Deterministic Infrastructure
↓
Reflection Systems
↓
Intelligence Systems

```

Lower layers may assist higher layers.

Lower layers never inherit higher-layer authority implicitly.

# Human Sovereignty Doctrine

Human governance remains authoritative.

Humans own:

* capital allocation
* production authorization
* freeze approval
* risk exposure
* architectural mutation approval
* deployment progression
* rollback authorization

The architecture rejects:

* AI sovereignty
* autonomous governance mutation
* self-authorizing infrastructure
* recursive operational authority

Human authority remains externalized and explicit.

---

# Approval Doctrine

All operationally meaningful mutations require explicit approval authority.

Examples:

* parameter adoption
* strategy deployment
* domain activation
* platform integration
* freeze transitions
* production rollout
* risk expansion

Approval must remain:

* explicit
* attributable
* reviewable
* auditable
* reversible

Implicit approval is prohibited.

---

# Freeze Governance Doctrine

Freeze authority preserves:

* architectural integrity
* deterministic guarantees
* operational stability
* bounded evolution
* lifecycle continuity

Frozen systems:

* must not mutate implicitly
* must not drift operationally
* must not inherit uncontrolled changes

Freeze violations are treated as architectural risk events.

---

# Rollback Doctrine

Rollback capability is mandatory.

All major operational mutations must preserve:

* rollback lineage
* reversibility
* deployment traceability
* freeze restoration capability

Rollback must remain:

* operationally feasible
* auditable
* bounded
* attributable

Irreversible operational mutation is prohibited.

---

# Risk Authorization Doctrine

Governance owns financial risk authorization.

Governance controls:

* exposure limits
* domain allocation
* strategy activation
* platform access
* execution permissions
* operational boundaries

No execution system may:

* expand risk autonomously
* redefine exposure constraints
* self-authorize scaling behavior

Risk authority remains externalized.

---

# Relationship With Deterministic Core

The deterministic core executes within approved governance constraints.

The deterministic core must NOT:

* mutate governance rules
* bypass approval gates
* self-authorize lifecycle transitions
* redefine risk exposure

Execution remains subordinate to governance.

---

# Relationship With Reflection Systems

Reflection systems may:

* propose adjustments
* identify governance risks
* summarize operational anomalies

Reflection systems do NOT:

* authorize deployment
* mutate governance rules
* bypass freeze authority

Reflection remains advisory.

---

# Relationship With Intelligence Systems

Intelligence systems may:

* assist governance analysis
* summarize operational risk
* evaluate lifecycle readiness
* assist audit workflows

Intelligence systems must NOT:

* self-authorize production mutation
* inherit governance authority
* redefine approval hierarchy
* bypass freeze discipline

Intelligence remains bounded.

---

# Governance Federation Philosophy

Governance may coordinate:

* multiple domains
* multiple strategies
* multiple intelligence systems
* multiple execution environments

Governance federation does NOT authorize:

* hidden orchestration
* authority diffusion
* implicit runtime coordination
* uncontrolled autonomy

Governance coordination remains explicit.

---

# Escalation Boundary Doctrine

Operational escalation paths must remain explicit.

Escalation triggers include:

* freeze violations
* abnormal drift
* execution instability
* observability failure
* uncontrolled operational mutation
* statistical integrity degradation

Escalation authority must remain attributable.

Hidden escalation behavior is prohibited.

---

# Bounded Autonomy Doctrine

Limited autonomy may exist only inside explicitly approved boundaries.

Autonomy may NEVER:

* redefine governance authority
* mutate freezes autonomously
* bypass rollback capability
* self-authorize production mutation
* inherit unrestricted operational control

Bounded autonomy is subordinate to governance authority.

---

# Governance Audit Philosophy

Governance systems must preserve:

* approval lineage
* deployment lineage
* freeze lineage
* rollback lineage
* operational lineage
* escalation lineage

All governance behavior must remain auditable.

Opaque authority is prohibited.

---

# Failure Philosophy

Governance failures must remain:

* explicit
* observable
* attributable
* reviewable

The system rejects:

* hidden authority mutation
* silent privilege escalation
* invisible deployment behavior
* implicit operational approval

Fail-fast governance behavior is mandatory.

---

# Operational Sovereignty Doctrine

Operational sovereignty remains internal.

External systems:

* do not own governance
* do not own lifecycle authority
* do not own architectural authority
* do not own freeze authority

The architecture must survive:

* platform replacement
* intelligence replacement
* tooling replacement
* dependency replacement

Governance remains internalized.

---

Governance systems must remain:

* explicit
* bounded
* understandable
* auditable
* reviewable
* operationally lightweight

The architecture rejects:

* governance middleware frameworks
* recursive governance intelligence
* hidden approval systems
* authority abstraction inflation
* probabilistic governance behavior

Complexity must justify itself continuously.
